<bound_statistic_decision>
**The verifier's `missing` bullet 1 names the triple product `rows x holiday_columns x
total_holiday_dates`. Task 1 must MEASURE whether that statistic can carry the bar, and Task 2 uses
whichever statistic the measurement supports. State the outcome explicitly; do not silently pick one.**

The reason for the doubt is arithmetic, and Task 1 turns it into evidence. Once holiday membership is
an O(1) `HashSet` lookup, the dates factor leaves the inner loop, and the triple product stops being
monotone in the real cost:

- `20 000 points x 1 000 columns x 1 date` = triple product **2.0e7**, but `rows x cols` = **2.0e7**
  cells — the expensive shape.
- `3 000 points x 181 columns x 84 dates` = triple product **4.56e7**, but `rows x cols` = **5.4e5**
  cells — 37x cheaper, yet 2.3x LARGER on the triple product.

A single threshold on the triple product therefore cannot both accept the cheap request and refuse the
expensive one — it would have to sit below 2.0e7 to refuse the first, which also refuses the second.
That is not a preference; it is a property of the statistic, and Task 1 proves it by measuring both
configurations and recording the two walls side by side.

The closure therefore bounds all three of the verifier's factors, on the statistic each one is
actually monotone in:

- `MAX_HOLIDAY_DESIGN_COST` on `(points + horizon) x holiday_columns` — the feature cells the request
  buys across `make_design` (points rows) AND `predict` (horizon rows), which is where the work is.
- `MAX_HOLIDAY_DATES_TOTAL` on `sum(len(h.dates))` across all holidays — the third factor, which after
  Task 1 is an ADDITIVE set-construction cost rather than a multiplier, and which is otherwise bounded
  only at 1e6 (up to 1 000 holidays, each contributing at least one column, each carrying up to
  `MAX_HOLIDAY_DATES` = 1 000 dates).

Both factors bounded means their product is bounded, which is the property the verifier asked for.
**If Task 1's measurements contradict the arithmetic above — if the design build turns out not to
dominate, or the triple product turns out to track the wall — then implement the literal triple-product
bound the verifier named and record the measurements that changed the answer.** The measurement
decides; this block only says what to measure and why.
</bound_statistic_decision>
