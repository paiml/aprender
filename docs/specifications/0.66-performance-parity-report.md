# 0.66 — Performance parity for 7B-class models: inference against llama.cpp and Ollama, fine-tuning against Unsloth

**Status:** DRAFT for the 0.66 cycle · written 2026-09-04 on the 0.65.0 release day · governing inference spec: `PP-LLAMA-001-MASTER.md` v3.1 (this document adds obligations, it does not replace §4–§7 of that one) · training spec of record: `aprender-train/entrenar-spec.md`, `aprender-train/ship-model-1-spec.md`

**Subject:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct at Q4_K_M (primary), with the rule applied to any dense 7–8B decoder (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) by the same protocol. **Comparators:** llama.cpp (pinned build, `llama-server`) and Ollama (pinned, same GGUF) for inference; Unsloth (pinned) for QLoRA fine-tuning. **Proof hosts:** `lambda` (RTX 4090, sm_89, 24 GB, ≈1,008 GB/s) and `gx10` (GB10 Blackwell, sm_121, 120 GB unified, ≈273 GB/s). A cell is proven only when both hosts carry a CONFORMANT ledger row (§8). 0.66 additionally makes **wgpu, Metal, AMD and wasm32 first-class backends** by the §2 definition, which adds two proof hosts — `intel` (x86 + Vulkan, the wgpu lane) and `mini` (Apple M4, the Metal lane) — and one CI-hosted lane (wasm32 under `wasmtime` and a browser). AMD has no host in the fleet and is therefore UNMEASURED with an owner and an acquisition anchor, never omitted (§1.5, §6.5).

Every figure carries its source. `[V]` verified by re-running the named command; `[X]` taken from a receipt or issue as written; `[C]` computed from `[V]`/`[X]` inputs with the arithmetic shown. Nothing here is a claim of parity; PP-12 forbids publishing a ratio that has no conformant receipt, and 0.65.0 ships no conformant receipt.

## 2. Definitions (inherited, then extended)

**Parity (inference).** PP-LLAMA-001 §4 verbatim: paired (same run, host, model, client, workload, window), named (cell, band, metric), asymmetric by band (c=1 gates `dec_ratio` and `prefill_ratio`; c>1 gates `agg_ratio`), correct before fast (PP-26), decision by the one-sided 95 % lower confidence bound `>= 1 − δ` with `δ = 0` (P-5), seeded at achieved (P-6), must-fire mutation per gate (P-7). 0.66 **adds** the qwen-coder-deploy bar as REPORTED at 0.66.0 and GATED at 0.67.0: TTFT p50 `<= 2×` and ITL p50 `<= 1.5×` the comparator at c=1; at c=4 aggregate `>= 3×` the c=1 decode of the same engine; at c=8 no OOM and no correctness loss.

**Parity (fine-tuning).** New. A QLoRA run is paired with an Unsloth run of the same base model, dataset, sequence length, micro-batch, LoRA rank/alpha/targets, steps, seed and host. Three metrics: `train_tok_per_sec` (gated, LCB95 `>= 1.0`), `peak_vram_bytes` (gated, `<= 1.0×`), and `final_loss` (a correctness witness: `|loss_apr − loss_unsloth| <= τ_loss` with `τ_loss` declared per dataset in the receipt; a run outside τ is INVALID-CORRECTNESS and its throughput is not reported). A fourth, `eval_delta`, is REPORTED: pass@1 or accuracy of the merged adapter on the held-out split, both engines.

**Ollama.** Ollama is llama.cpp behind a daemon. Its decode numbers historically match llama.cpp's within noise on lambda (300.7 / 299.9 / 328.6 / 313.5 tok/s over six weeks on the 1.5B `[X]`), so Ollama is a **second lane on the same comparator pin**, reported at c=1 only, and gated only on the CLI wall-clock workload W5 (§6, W-F) where its resident daemon is the thing users feel. (not a measurement artifact — quoted from a doc, not from an evidence artifact; provenance: evidence/reports/0.66-parity-report-provenance.json)

**First-class backend.** New, and the definition the rest of this document is measured against. A
backend is **first-class** when all seven of the following hold. Fewer than seven is not "nearly
first-class"; it is a backend with a named gap and an owner.

1. **Compiled and tested in CI on a host that has the device.** Not "it compiles"; a job that runs on
   silicon that can execute it. `wgpu` fails this today on every host (§1.5 B).
2. **The refusal is per-backend.** `apr run --backend <x>` / `apr serve run --backend <x>` exits
   non-zero with one line when `<x>` is absent from *this* binary — not when *some* accelerator is
   absent. Today the check is `cfg!(any(feature = "cuda", feature = "wgpu"))` (`accel.rs:28`,
   `serve/mod.rs:236`) `[V]`, so it passes a `--backend cuda` request on a wgpu-only build: #2696
   surviving inside its own fix.
3. **`scripts/perf-matrix.yaml` carries a cell for it** with a `compute_class` the binary can actually
   take (PP-16: the #2841 rule — never declare a class no build reaches), an `owner`, and exactly one
   of `expires` / `expires_after`.
4. **Both witnesses run on it**: the PP-26 batch-invariance witness (`min_agree_tokens: 64`,
   `max_constant_run: 16`) and the golden-output witness, on the device, in the nightly.
5. **The post-publish dogfood on that host records the backend it used** — the value, not the request
   (`feedback_verification_discipline` #2: prove the mechanism engaged).
6. **The same model formats load**: GGUF, APR and SafeTensors, or the exclusions are enumerated in the
   receipt with a reason.
7. **A pinned comparator**, or — where none exists — a self-ratchet seeded at achieved with the
   `comparator` field written `UNMEASURED{owner}`. Never an absent field, never an estimate.

Under this definition the score at `587ad0797` is: **cuda 4/7** (1 ✓ `cuda-nightly.yml`; 2 partial —
`dispatch.rs:173` only, only `apr run`; 3 ✓ UNMEASURED on both hosts; 4 ✓; 5 ✗ #2696; 6 ✓; 7 ✓),
**wgpu 1/7**, **Metal 0/7**, **AMD 0/7**, **wasm32 0/7** `[C]` from §1.5. 0.66 takes wgpu, Metal and
wasm32 to 7/7 and gives AMD an owned, dated cell rather than silence.

**The migration rule.** A `[lib] name` change is a **breaking change for every `use trueno::…` path
in the workspace and in every downstream crate**, published or not. It is therefore not a rename
commit; it is a migration with a compatibility window:

1. **Reserve the crates.io names first.** The target package names are already ours (`aprender-*` are
   the published packages), but any *new* shim package name is reserved **before** the rename PR opens.
   A rename that lands against an unreserved name is a publish cascade that fails halfway
   (`feedback_crates_io_cascade_auth_config_traps`).
2. **One PR per crate.** Not one PR per group and never a workspace-wide sed: a 51-crate rename in one
   commit cannot be reviewed, cannot be bisected, and (per `feedback_two_concurrent_workspace_tests_starve_the_box`)
   would occupy the merge queue for a day per attempt. Each PR renames one `[lib] name`, updates every
   `use` in the workspace, and lands green.
3. **A `pub use` compatibility shim under the old name for one population, and an in-crate alias
   for the other.** Two different downstreams break, and one shim does not serve both — the shim is a
   *package*, so it only reaches consumers who have an edge to that package:
   - **Consumers of the OLD published package** (`trueno = "…"` in their manifest) are served by the
     shim. Crate `trueno` becomes a shim whose entire content is `pub use aprender_compute::*;` with a
     `#[deprecated]` note naming the replacement and the removal version.
   - **Consumers of the CURRENT package** (`aprender-compute = "…"`) who write `use trueno::…`
     because that is today's `[lib] name` are **not** reached by the shim: they have no dependency
     edge to a package called `trueno`, and their paths break the moment the `[lib] name` changes.
     For this population the rename ships either (a) a `#[deprecated] pub mod trueno { pub use
     crate::*; }` alias **inside** `aprender_compute` for the same two-minor-version window, or (b) an
     announced hard breaking change with the minor bump. (a) is the default; (b) is permitted only if
     the row says so in writing.
   - **In-workspace consumers** are already handled by rule 2: each PR updates every `use` in the
     workspace and lands green.

   Two minor versions is the window; each removal is its own PR and its own row. UNMEASURED: how many
   external downstreams sit in each of the first two populations — this was written with no network
   access on 2026-09-04, owner `spec-owner`. The count does not change the rule, only the size of the
   announcement, so no rename PR is blocked on it.
4. **A rename guard, `scripts/check_crate_names.sh`**, whose universe is the **workspace member set
   plus the workspace root** — mechanically, `git ls-files '*/Cargo.toml' 'Cargo.toml'` **union** a
   working-tree `find` for the same pattern (`feedback_tracked_only_universe_gives_a_free_pass`: a
   tracked-only universe gives an untracked new crate a free pass) — and fails when a `[lib] name` is
   not the directory with `-` → `_`, with an explicit allow-list of exactly the rows still in flight
   and the `aprender-core` decision row. **`crates/*/Cargo.toml` is not the universe**, and the two
   manifests it drops are exactly the interesting ones (`feedback_guard_universe_from_wrong_side`: ask
   what the loop iterates and whether the defect removes items from it): it excludes the workspace
   **root** `Cargo.toml`, which declares `[package] name = "aprender"` and `[lib] name = "aprender"`,
   `path = "src/lib.rs"` `[V]` at `587ad0797` — the same `[lib] name` as `crates/aprender-core`, i.e.
   the one row this document defers — and it misses `crates/facades/*/Cargo.toml` at depth two (three
   manifests, conformant today `[V]`). It ships with:
   - a **case table** of must-match / must-not-match inputs (`name = "trueno"` under
     `crates/aprender-compute` ⇒ FAIL; `name="aprender_compute"` with no spaces ⇒ PASS; a commented-out
     `# name = "trueno"` ⇒ PASS; a `[lib]` block absent ⇒ PASS, the default is the package name; a
     second `[[bin]] name` ⇒ ignored; the **workspace root** `Cargo.toml` with `[lib] name =
     "aprender"` ⇒ scanned and reported under the `aprender-core` DECISION REQUIRED row, never
     silently PASS by falling outside the glob; `crates/facades/provable-contracts/Cargo.toml` at
     depth two ⇒ scanned, and PASSES today because `provable_contracts` is its package name with
     underscores `[V]`; the same `[lib] name` declared by two manifests in one workspace ⇒ reported,
     and allowed only by an allow-list entry that names the decision row it is waiting on) — the
     guard-regex case table is mandatory here because the
     `apr`-invocation patterns were wrong five times and every one was caught by a table, none by
     review (`feedback_verification_discipline` #7);
   - a **must-fire mutation**: re-introducing any one row's old `[lib] name` must turn the guard RED,
     demonstrated in the guard's own PR;
   - **fail-closed** behaviour: a guard that cannot parse a manifest exits non-zero.
5. **The guard lands BEFORE the first rename** and with its allow-list full, so the ratchet is
   "51 remaining → 0" and every rename PR shrinks a number in the same commit that changes the code
   (`feedback_guard_and_its_classification_same_commit`).

**Cost, stated rather than hidden.** UNMEASURED: the number of `use <old>::` sites in the workspace
per crate has not been counted, owner `spec-owner`, and no rename PR is sequenced until it is —
sequencing 51 PRs by an unmeasured size is the same guess this document exists to refuse.

## 7. Workloads

W1 (closed-loop chat, 55 prompts, streaming) and W2 (interference) as PP-LLAMA-001 §5.1. 0.66 adds:

- **W3** open-loop at a fixed request rate (latency bound, §12 row 16; still 2026-10-23).
- **W4** prompt-token sweep {64, 256, 512, 2048} at c=1 (prefill decomposition, §12 row 17).
- **W5** CLI wall-clock: `apr run` / `ollama run` / `llama-cli` for a 32-token completion, cold and resident, 20 replicates (G7).
- **WT** training: the T-0 recipe above, one receipt per (engine, host).
- **W6** wasm decode: `qwen2.5-coder-0.5b` Q4_K_M loaded and decoded under `wasmtime` and under a
  headless browser, 32-token greedy completion, paired in the same job with the native CPU lane on the
  same host; the artifact's byte size is part of the receipt (B-S1..B-S3, §6.5).
- `W1` is not duplicated per backend: a wgpu, Metal or wasm run is **W1 with the backend named in the
  cell key** (`intel/W1_wgpu`, `mini/W1_metal`), so the protocol, the ladder and the witnesses are the
  ones already declared in `perf-matrix.yaml` rather than a second set that can drift.

## 8. Proof — what "gx10 and lambda prove it works" means

A workstream is DONE when, and only when, all of the following hold on **both** hosts:

1. A `CONFORMANT` row in `evidence/parity/LEDGER.md` (Appendix C schema): signature, `compute_class` and the §5.2 effective-config as reported by the server, comparator pin with expiry state, `slots_admitted` on both lanes, `started_utc`/`clock_source`, streaming witness, **PP-26 witness PASS on every band in the ladder**, `short_of_n_predict`, client sha256, roofline against per-sequence decode.
2. `n >= 5` interleaved replicates, both lanes, one harness invocation, one commit, clocks locked (`nvidia-smi -lgc` on lambda; the GB10 equivalent recorded in the receipt).
3. The gated metrics for the band PASS P-5 with `δ = 0`, and the gate is ARMED from that receipt (`armed_by` set).
4. For training, the WT receipt with the three gated metrics and the loss witness, both hosts.
5. The receipt is produced by `scripts/perf_gate.sh --phase release` on the release host and the cell is not re-run to green (PP-9: a cell once run is spent; a second run is a new row that says why).

A workstream with a lambda row and no gx10 row is **LANDED, not PROVEN**, and `perf-matrix.yaml` keeps the gx10 cell UNMEASURED with owner and expiry. The same rule
extends to the backends of §6.5, with "both hosts" read as **the hosts that carry that backend's
device**: cuda ⇒ `lambda` + `gx10`; wgpu ⇒ `intel` (plus any AMD host once acquired); Metal ⇒ `mini`,
where the two lanes are llama.cpp-Metal **and** Ollama; wasm32 ⇒ the CI runner, where the two lanes are
`wasmtime` **and** the browser harness. So: B-W4 with an `intel` row and no AMD row is LANDED, not
PROVEN, and the AMD cell stays UNMEASURED against its acquisition anchor; B-M3 with a llama.cpp-Metal
row and no Ollama row is LANDED, not PROVEN; B-S with a `wasmtime` row and no browser row is LANDED,
not PROVEN. A backend is FIRST-CLASS (§2) only when all seven clauses hold, which is strictly more
than PROVEN: PROVEN is clause 7's receipt, and clauses 1-6 are the ones that keep it true next month. The hardware window is the binding constraint; the spec-owner schedules gx10 windows in the order W-G, W-B, W-E, T-0, T-1, W-H.

## 9. Non-goals and refusals

- No re-arming of `HW_DP4A_Q4K` or `FUSED_GATE_UP=1` (F2 correctness floor; PP-LLAMA-001 §10 refusals).
- No published ratio without a CONFORMANT receipt (PP-12). No "≈96 % of llama.cpp" prose (book chapter 22) until a receipt names the cell.
- No comparator configured away from its documented default except the pinned argv in `llama_pin.toml`; batching is never turned off on the comparator to make a band pass (PP-3).
- No CPU-class training claims; MODEL-class training ships GPU only (`feedback_model_1_ships_gpu_only`).
- ~~No Metal cell (`mini` is NA, #2841) in 0.66.~~ **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-04 by §6.5 B-M** (decider:
  spec-owner). The NA was correct on 2026-09-02 for the reason given — no build in the tree reached a
  Metal path `[V]` — and it stops being correct the moment B-M1 lands one. `mini` becomes
  `UNMEASURED{owner: spec-owner, expires: 2026-10-17}` **when the path exists and not before**; the NA
  is not deleted from the cell, it is superseded in place with its date and its decider, so the record
  of why the host was excluded survives the change.
- **AMD is not a non-goal; it is UNMEASURED with an owner.** There is no AMD device in the fleet
  (`lambda` 4090, `gx10` GB10, `intel` x86/Vulkan, `mini` M4) `[V]`, so 0.66 cannot measure it. What
  0.66 refuses is the *silence*: the vendor gets a cell, an owner (`spec-owner`) and an expiry anchored
  on hardware acquisition (B-A1), and its path is the same vendor-neutral wgpu-Vulkan lane the `intel`
  work builds. ROCm/HIP is a later obligation with the host acquisition as its declared blocker, not a
  refusal.
- No wgpu or Metal number taken through a **software rasteriser** (`lavapipe`, `llvmpipe`) or through
  an adapter the receipt does not name. "Prove the mechanism engaged": a `Backend: WGPU` banner is a
  request, not a device (`feedback_verification_discipline` #2).
- No **native Metal kernels** in 0.66 unless the wgpu-on-Metal lane misses parity first (B-M1). A
  second kernel family is a new PP-26 obligation before it is a speed-up; §4 G6 already carries three
  kernel families that disagree.
- No **`[lib]` rename landed without its guard and its shim** (§6.7): a rename that breaks
  `use trueno::…` for downstream users without a deprecation window is a published breaking change,
  and this document does not authorise one.
- vLLM is not a 0.66 comparator; the prior art's c≥4 vLLM bar is deferred to 0.67 once W-E exists.

**The `owner` column.** Every row names an owner *here*, in the DAG, not only once it is already
past its expiry in `perf-matrix.yaml`. This document names exactly two owner identities, and the
column uses them by a stated rule so a reader can audit the assignment rather than trust it:
`perf-gate` owns the rows whose artifact is a measurement, a receipt, a `perf-matrix.yaml` cell or an
armed gate; `spec-owner` owns the rows whose artifact is code, a guard, a document, a naming decision
or a matrix schema entry. Where the body of this document already names an owner for a row, the column
repeats that owner and does not override it (rows 21, 27, 28, 33, 34, 36). This column is the owner of
record for the workstream ids of §5, §6 and §6.5 as well; those tables carry the id, this one carries
the owner. Which *person or team* answers to each identity is not decided by this document.

**Rows 17, 18 and 35 are the release criteria for 0.66**: 17 and 18 are the two parity cells, and 35
is the statement that the backends this document promoted are first-class by the §2 definition rather
than by announcement. Rows past their expiry are UNMEASURED with an owner in `perf-matrix.yaml` and
block the 0.66 tag under PP-6 exactly as today; nothing is fabricated to make a date.

**What row 35 is blocked by, and what it is deliberately not blocked by.** Row 35's blockers are the
rows that establish first-class status per the seven clauses of §2 — 20–24, 26, 27, 29–33 (the
refusal, the lanes, the comparators, the witnesses, the receipts) and 37–39 (clauses 5 and 6 per
backend). Two rows are **not** blockers of it, and the earlier blanket "20–34" wrongly made them so:

- **Row 28** (`cuda-backend-architecture.md`) is gated on a third-party server's reachability
  (`ETIMEOUT api.copilot.nsight.ngc.nvidia.com`, 2026-09-04, recorded with its error string). A
  documentation obligation whose blocker is someone else's DNS must not gate a parity release
  criterion; it keeps its own row, its own expiry and its RED-with-`UNVERIFIED`-count rule (§6.6), on
  a track that does not block the tag.
- **Row 34** (§6.7 renames executed) is sized by a count §6.7 declares UNMEASURED — "the number of
  `use <old>::` sites … has not been counted … and no rename PR is sequenced until it is". An
  unsequenceable body of 51 PRs cannot gate the tag; row 34 keeps its expiry and its owner on the same
  non-release-blocking track. What *does* gate the tag is that no rename lands without its guard and
  its shim (§9), and row 25 (the guard) remains on the blocking side through the rows that need it.

Both rows still go RED at their expiry with their owner; RED on this track is a finding against the
owner, not a hold on the 0.66 tag.

**Expiry slack.** No row expires on the same day as a row it blocks. The three zero-slack pairs the
first draft carried (13→14, and 33→35 and 34→35) are corrected here: T-2 and T-3 move a week earlier

minimum slack in the table is now six days (rows 14, 15 → 18; rows 31, 32, 33, 37–39 → 35; row 31 → 36); a release
criterion is never scheduled to be satisfied on the day its last input is due.
