## Verdict: GO, and the rule that produced it

**Every one of the six defects is present at `v0.66.0`.** Measured, not assumed:

| defect | evidence it predates the cut |
|---|---|
| `aprender-zram-core` cuda never compiled (#3179) | `src/gpu/batch/mod.rs` names `PinnedBuffer` 5× at `v0.66.0` and 5× at the cut |
| `hw_dp4a_q6k` uninitialised buffer (#3178) | the `expect` is on line 356 of `weight.rs` at both refs |
| `llama2-train` cwd-relative config (#3180) | lines 12, 233, 364 identical at both refs |
| `performance_parity` Q4_0 length (#3181) | unchanged since before `v0.66.0` |
| `coop_gemm_bench` | deleted on `main` by #3136, ahead of this cut |

APR-RELEASE-001 §3.1 skips a train whose **cut** cannot go green. None of these
was caused by the cut: the previous tag shipped all of them, under a gate that
did not exist then. A gate running for the first time discovers a backlog; it
does not retroactively make the backlog this train's fault. The Toyota reading is
to surface, file and ratchet — which is what the five tickets above are — not to
hold a release hostage to the moment a new instrument was switched on.

What this verdict is **not**: it is not a claim that the 20 timeouts are healthy.
They were not individually verified. They are named in #3182 and the gate cannot
tell a serving server from a wedged one until that lands. The next train measures
them.

**The ratchet from here:** 0.68.0 lands #3182's classes and #3163's `needs-data`,
so the 0.68.0 sweep reports genuine defects only. A `fail` row in that run, on a
tree whose previous tag was clean, is a train-stopping RED with no argument.
